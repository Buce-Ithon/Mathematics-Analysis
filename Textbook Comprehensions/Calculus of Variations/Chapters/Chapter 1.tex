\subsection{Basics summary}
The objects what we mainly studies throught out the book: \\
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.95\textwidth}
    \begin{df*}
    Given a set $M$ of functions, the map $I : M \to \mathbb{C}$ is called a \textbf{functional}.
    \end{df*}
\end{minipage}}
\newline\newline
Some example to tell if it a map is a functional: \\
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.95\textwidth}
    \begin{ex*}
        $I_1(u) = \max_{x\in\Omega}|u(x)|$, $I_2(u) = u(x_0)$, $I_3(u)=\int_{\Omega}[|\nabla u(x)|^2-F(u(x))]dx$ are all functional.
    \end{ex*}
    \begin{ex*}
        $\forall M \ \text{and} \ f : \mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, $I_4(u) = f(u(x))$ is not functional.
    \end{ex*}
\end{minipage}}
\newline\newline
A typical example problem to introduce the subject what we studies: 
\begin{prb}
    (Brachistochrone) Given two points $A = (x_1, y_1)$ and $B = (x_2, y_2)$ in a vertical plane, where $x_1 < x_2$, $y_1 > y_2$. A particle slides along a smooth curve connecting these two points under gravity alone. Assuming initial velocity is zero, ask along which curve the sliding time is the shortest
\end{prb}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.95\textwidth}
    \begin{sol*}
        Let $u \in C^1[x_1, x_2]$, ${(x, u(x)) \mid x \in [x_1, x_2], u(x_i) = y_i, i = 1, 2}$ is a curve connecting $A, B$. Since we have
        $\begin{cases} \frac{1}{2} mv^2 = mgh, \\ v = \frac{ds}{dt}, \end{cases}$
        so
        $v = \sqrt{2g(y_1 - u(x))}$,
        and
        $dt = \frac{ds}{v} = \sqrt{\frac{1 + |u'(x)|^2}{2g(y_1 - u(x))}} dx$,
        total time
        $T = \int_{x_0}^{x_1} dt = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2g}} \int_{x_0}^{x_1} \sqrt{\frac{1 + |u'(x)|^2}{y_1 - u(x)}} dx$.

        Let
        $M = {u \in C^1([x_1, x_2]) \mid u(x_i) = y_i, i = 1, 2}$,
        then the mapping
        $M \to \mathbb{R}, \quad u \mapsto T$
        is a "functional". Here $u$ is the independent variable, $T = T(u)$ is the dependent variable. We want to find $u$ in $M$ such that $T$ is minimized.
    \end{sol*}
\end{minipage}}
